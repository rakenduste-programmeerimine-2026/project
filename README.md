# Project Phase: Eligibility to Participate

The project phase starts on **5 November** and is open only to students who have **completed and successfully submitted all required homework by the applicable deadlines**.

Meeting these requirements is a prerequisite for entering the project phase and continuing towards completion of the course.

If any homework is missing, late or not yet accepted, contact the teacher before 5 November to clarify your eligibility and any available next steps.

The assignment below applies to students who have fulfilled these requirements.

# Project Phase: Two Small Apps for Real Users

Build two small applications that solve real problems for real people. Then choose one to improve and present.

**This README is the official source of assignment requirements.** Discord is used for announcements, questions and coordination.

## Schedule

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Before the lesson on 5 November | Form your team, prepare two ideas and confirm your contacts |
| 5–18 November | Build and submit Project 1, including user feedback |
| 19 November–2 December | Build and submit Project 2, with rotated roles |
| 3–9 December | Choose your stronger app, improve it and prepare the presentation |
| 10 December | Final presentations |

**Development starts on 5 November. No coding is required beforehand.**

During development, expect three hours in class and approximately three hours of homework per person each week.

## 1. Form a Team of Three

Stay together for both projects. Choose your starting roles:

| Role | Main responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Frontend & UX | Pages, forms, styling and connecting the interface to the backend |
| Backend & security | Endpoints, application logic, validation and access checks |
| Database & quality | Supabase, database access policies, testing and deployment setup |

For Project 2, rotate:

**Frontend → Backend → Database & quality → Frontend**

Everyone contributes technically and understands how the application works. Planning and coordination are shared responsibilities.

In December, each member takes one small improvement task in the role they have not yet tried.

## 2. Prepare Two Small App Ideas

Each project should have:

- One main user journey.
- A maximum of three core features.
- Approximately one or two database tables.

Use **Next.js and Supabase**, unless another approach is agreed with the teacher.

Choose something achievable within two weeks alongside your other courses. A small, reliable app that helps someone is the goal.

## 3. A Real Contact Is Mandatory for Each Project

**Every project must have an identifiable person who experiences the problem or represents its intended users.**

A fictional client, AI-generated persona or “students in general” is not enough.

Your contact can be a student, club member, friend, family member or representative of an organisation. A paying client is not required.

The same person may be the contact for both projects if they genuinely experience both problems.

Before committing to an idea, confirm that your contact agrees to:

1. Explain their problem and current workflow.
2. Try your prototype before it is presented.
3. Participate in a short recorded feedback conversation.
4. Allow the recording to be shown to the teacher and classmates.
5. Allow an agreed contact method to be shared privately with the teacher if clarification is needed.

Be ready to provide the contact’s name, connection to the project and agreed contact method privately on request.

**Do not publish private contact details in GitHub, Discord or slides.**

If your contact cannot agree to these arrangements, speak to the teacher before committing to the idea.

## 4. Have an Initial Conversation

Before 5 November, ask your contact:

1. When did you last experience this problem?
2. How do you handle it currently?
3. What is frustrating or time-consuming?
4. What is the smallest improvement that would help?
5. What should our prototype let you do for it to count as useful?

Write down two or three findings.

Agree on one simple success criterion. For example:

> A user can save a course link and find it again without searching through chat.

**The initial conversation does not need to be recorded. The later prototype feedback conversation must be recorded.**

## 6. What Happens on 5 November?

The teacher will provide access to repositories in the course GitHub organisation.

In class, we will:

1. Pitch the ideas.
2. Confirm the contacts and realistic project scope.
3. Agree on roles and responsibilities.
4. Set up the repositories and development workflow.
5. Break the work into manageable tasks.
6. Start building.

Detailed technical setup instructions and AI-assisted coding rules will be provided in class.

You do not need to prepare repositories or development plans beforehand.

## 7. What Is Expected During Development?

For each project:

- Build the agreed core features.
- Contribute technically and collaborate with your teammates.
- Keep track of responsibilities and progress.
- Integrate your work regularly.
- Validate inputs and protect data appropriately.
- Keep credentials and secrets out of Git.
- Test important behaviour.
- Maintain clear setup instructions.
- Be ready to explain your code and decisions, including AI-assisted work.

At the midpoint, demonstrate one working end-to-end feature: an action in the interface that reaches the backend and database.

Roles describe your main responsibility, not the only work you may do.

## 8. Mandatory User Test and Recorded Feedback

**Both projects require a user test and recorded feedback.**

Complete this before each project’s presentation or submission, whichever comes first:

- **Project 1:** No later than 18 November.
- **Project 2:** No later than 2 December.

### Run the Session

1. Remind the contact of the original problem and success criterion.
2. Briefly demonstrate the prototype.
3. Ask them to attempt one realistic task themselves where possible.
4. Observe where they succeed or need help.
5. Ask the feedback questions below.
6. Agree on the most useful improvement.

Do not coach the contact into giving positive answers.

### Feedback Questions

1. What problem were you hoping the app would solve?
2. Were you able to complete the agreed task? Where did you need help?
3. Does it solve the problem fully, partly or not yet? Why?
4. Would you actually use it? Why or why not?
5. What was most useful, confusing or missing?
6. Is it worth developing further? What should change first?
7. Overall, how well did the result meet what we agreed at the beginning?

### Recording Requirements

- Obtain permission before recording.
- Explain that the recording will be shown to the teacher and classmates.
- Audio or video is acceptable; appearing on camera is not required.
- Aim for **three to five minutes of feedback**.
- Prepare a **60–90-second excerpt** for classroom presentation.
- Keep the full recording available to the teacher through a restricted link.
- Preserve the meaning of the feedback when editing.
- Do not upload recordings to public repositories or public channels.
- Avoid recording credentials, sensitive data or unrelated personal information.

If recording becomes impossible or permission is withdrawn, contact the teacher before the deadline to agree on an alternative. Do not record secretly.

### Respond to the Feedback

Document:

- What the contact confirmed.
- What did not work or was missing.
- What you changed.
- What remains unfinished.

Make at least one small improvement based on the feedback. If it cannot fit the remaining time, explain why and record a clearly scoped follow-up task.

**Positive feedback is not required for a good result. Honest evidence, understanding and a thoughtful response matter.**

## 9. December: Choose and Improve One App

Choose the stronger of your two projects.

Use 3–9 December to:

- Fix bugs.
- Improve usability and reliability.
- Check security and important behaviour.
- Prepare the demonstration.

Do not add major new features.

Each member takes one small improvement task in the role they have not yet tried.

**Both projects still need their own feedback recording, even though only one is chosen for the final full presentation.**

If the chosen app changes substantially after its feedback session, ask the contact to try the updated part and confirm whether it improved.

## 10. Final Presentation on 10 December

Prepare **three to five slides and a live demonstration**.

Cover:

1. The intended user, original problem and success criterion.
2. Your working application.
3. How the frontend, backend and database connect.
4. The recorded contact feedback excerpt.
5. What you changed, what remains unfinished and whether you will continue.

Every member explains their contribution and what they learned from changing roles.

Represent the contact’s feedback accurately, including criticism.

## 11. Submission Checklist

For **each project**, submit:

- Repository link.
- Setup instructions and example environment configuration without secrets.
- README explaining the problem, scope and success criterion.
- Evidence of each member’s contribution.
- Relevant tests or documented checks and their results.
- User-test and feedback summary.
- A restricted link to the full feedback recording, shared privately with the teacher.
- Evidence of an improvement or a justified follow-up task.
- List of completed and unfinished requirements.

For the **final presentation**, also submit:

- Slides.
- Working demonstration.
- Selected feedback excerpt.

## What You Need to Do Now

Before 5 November:

1. Form a team of three.
2. Choose starting roles.
3. Brainstorm two small ideas.
4. Confirm a real contact for each idea.
5. Have the initial conversations.
6. Submit your short proposal.

**No coding, repository setup or detailed technical planning is required yet.**
